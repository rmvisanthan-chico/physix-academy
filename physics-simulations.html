Interactive Physics Simulations

40 free simulations you operate yourself — no download, no login, no ads. Run them in the browser, change the numbers, and watch the physics respond.

A simulation is not a video. In every PhysiX Academy simulation you set the inputs — angle, mass, voltage, frequency, focal length — and the experiment plays out from those values. That is the difference between reading that R = v² sin 2θ / g and watching the range grow when you raise the launch angle, then shrink again as you pass 45°.

Interactive lab benches

Three hand-built instrument benches: every knob, meter and dial is drawn and wired to the live physics. Turn the controls and the machine answers.

Ripple tank

Two dippers tap a water tank and a strobe lamp projects the interference pattern — bright crest lines and grey nodal lines — onto the paper below. Turn the FREQUENCY and SPACING knobs.

Spring, mass and damper

A brass mass hangs on a coil spring over an oil dashpot. Set MASS, STIFFNESS and DAMPING, press RELEASE, and the dial tracks the live displacement while the readout shows the damping ratio.

Common questions

Are the simulations free?

Yes. Every simulation, lesson, formula and calculator on PhysiX Academy is free, with no ads and no paywall. Read more about why it is free.

Do I need an account?

No. There is no sign-up wall. Progress is stored locally in your browser, so the site works offline too.

Do they work on a phone?

Yes. The simulations are built to run on desktop and mobile browsers, and they adapt to the screen you are using.

Are these the same as PhET?

No. PhET is a great general-purpose library; PhysiX Academy is tied directly to the NCERT Class 9–12 chapters and written to match that sequence. See the full comparison.
