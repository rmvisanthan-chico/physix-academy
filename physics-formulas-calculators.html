Physics Formulas & Calculators

Every formula you need for Class 9–12, with each symbol and unit written out — plus calculators that show the substitution instead of hiding it.

A formula is a sentence about how quantities relate. Written alone, F = ma is three letters; written with its units and its conditions, it tells you that doubling the mass at the same force halves the acceleration, and that it only holds in an inertial frame. The formula library is organised the second way.

What is covered

Mechanics

Kinematics, Newton's laws, momentum, work, energy, power, circular and rotational motion, gravitation.

Properties of matter

Elasticity, stress and strain, pressure, buoyancy, Bernoulli's principle, surface tension.

Heat & thermodynamics

Calorimetry, expansion, the gas laws, kinetic theory, the first law and efficiency.

Waves & sound

Wave equation, superposition, standing waves, resonance, the Doppler effect.

Optics

Mirror and lens formulas, magnification, refraction, the eye, interference and diffraction.

Electricity & magnetism

Coulomb's law, Ohm's law, series and parallel, capacitors, magnetic force, induction and AC.

Modern physics

Photoelectric equation, Bohr model, nuclear decay, mass–energy equivalence.

Constants

g, G, c, e, h, kB, NA and the values used by the site's own simulations.

Calculators that show their work

Each calculator takes the quantities you know, applies the relationship, and prints the substitution. You see the numbers go in, so a sign error or a unit slip is visible instead of hidden behind a result.

One source of truth

The same relationship definitions power the formula library, the calculators, the tutor and the simulations. When a simulation shows T = 2π√(l/g), that is the same expression the library lists and the calculator evaluates — they cannot drift apart.
