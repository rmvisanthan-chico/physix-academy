Case study: an offline physics tutor

No API key, no model download, no server. A tutor that runs in the browser and prints every step so a student can check it.

The problem

A student stuck on a numerical question usually wants one thing: the next step. Chat-based tutors are good at producing confident prose, but a confident wrong answer in physics is worse than no answer — it teaches the error. Most of these tools also need an account, a connection and someone else's server to run.

Constraints

  • Free, with no per-question cost for the site owner
  • Works offline after the first visit
  • No question ever leaves the student's device
  • Every printed step must be verifiable by hand

The approach

Instead of a language model, the tutor is a set of pure functions over parsed input. A question is read for the quantities it names and the relationship it implies; the solver then selects the matching expression, substitutes the values and prints the sequence.

Each relationship carries its own conditions and sign conventions. The lens solver, for example, states which side of the lens the image is on and gives the sign, rather than returning a bare number. When a question falls outside the supported set, the tutor says so instead of improvising.

What is covered today

Ohm's law and series/parallel resistance, mirror and thin-lens problems with magnification, uniform-acceleration kinematics, kinetic energy, and echo distance from a time delay. The list grows in batches, and each addition is tested against worked textbook examples.

The trade-off

A constrained solver is less flexible than a general chatbot: it cannot discuss a topic freely, and it will refuse anything outside its set. In exchange, it never invents a number, it costs nothing to run, and the entire interaction stays local. For homework checking, that trade is the right one.

What it shares with the rest of the site

The tutor uses the same relationship definitions as the formula library, the calculators and the simulations. That single source of truth is why a pendulum simulation, a formula entry and a tutor answer cannot disagree about the period of a pendulum.
