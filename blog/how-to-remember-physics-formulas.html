How to remember physics formulas without forgetting them

By R.M. Visanthan · 3 September 2026

There's a reason students forget formulas a week after learning them: they tried to memorise the formula by itself, floating free from any meaning. Here's a system that makes formulas stick.

1. Derive, don't memorise

The single most powerful trick. When you derive a formula from a definition or a law, it becomes a consequence, not a random string of symbols. "v = u + at" is just the definition of acceleration rearranged. Derive "s = ut + ½at²" by integrating — and you'll never forget it.

2. Understand what each symbol means physically

Every symbol stands for a real thing: u is "how fast you started", a is "how fast the speed changes each second". When you translate symbols into these plain-language meanings, the formula starts telling a story.

3. Check the dimensions

Every term in a valid physics formula must have the same units. This is both a memory aid and an error-checker. Try to see why "½at²" is a length — it's half of (acceleration × time × time).

4. Group by ideas, not alphabetically

Don't memorise formulas in a flat list. Group them by what they describe: all the motion equations together, all the energy equations together. The links between them (e.g., energy is the integral of force) help you recall whole families at once.

5. Use spaced repetition

Your brain forgets on a schedule. Review a formula after 1 day, then 3 days, then a week, then a month — and it moves into long-term memory. A formula sheet you review on that schedule beats one you cram the night before.

6. Attach a mental image

For each formula, recall the simulation or diagram that shows it. The projectile formula is the arc you watched fly. Memory loves pictures — use them.
