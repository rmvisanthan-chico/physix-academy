Top 10 mistakes students make in physics (and how to fix them)

By R.M.VISANTHAN · 3 September 2026

Most physics marks are lost not to hard questions, but to repeated small errors. Here are the ten most common ones — and the habit that fixes each.

1. Losing the sign

In kinematics and electrostatics, directions matter. A vector without a sign is just half an answer. Fix: always draw a coordinate axis and label directions before calculating.

2. Mixing up units

Using centimetres in one place and metres in another silently breaks an answer. Fix: convert everything to SI base units first thing.

3. Using the wrong formula

Students memorise formulas but not their conditions. v² = u² + 2as only works for constant acceleration. Fix: before applying a formula, state its assumption (e.g., "constant acceleration").

4. Skipping the free-body diagram

In mechanics, the diagram isn't decoration — it's the problem. Forgetting a force (friction, normal, tension) sends the whole answer wrong. Fix: never solve a force problem without drawing all forces first.

5. Rounding too early

Rounding intermediate values compounds errors. Fix: keep full precision until the final step, then round once.

6. Ignoring the question's format

The answer might need to be in kW, not W; in km/h, not m/s. Fix: re-read what units the question wants before it asks you to submit.

7. Not checking limiting cases

Quick sanity tests catch mistakes. If a mirror problem gives infinite focal length, something's off. Fix: after an answer, test an obvious extreme (zero, infinity, equal values).

8. Confusing weight and mass

Mass is the amount of matter (kg); weight is a force (N). They're different quantities. Fix: wherever you see "weight", write it as m·g and treat it as a force.

9. Memorising instead of deriving

Rote formulas fail on novel questions. If you can derive a formula, you can handle any twist. Fix: derive each formula once by hand from a definition.

10. Not reading the explanation when wrong

Getting an answer wrong is only valuable if you understand why. Fix: always read the full explanation for a missed question — it turns a mistake into a mark gained.
