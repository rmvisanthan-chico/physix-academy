How to study physics without memorising (and actually understand it)

By R.M.VISANTHAN · 3 September 2026

The biggest mistake students make with physics is treating it like a memorisation subject. They cram formulas, definitions and "standard answers" — then hit a question phrased slightly differently and freeze.

Physics is a reasoning subject. The formulas are the end of the process, not the beginning. Here's a method that works.

1. Start with a question, not the formula

Every topic began as a real question: "Why does a rocket work in space?", "Why is the sky blue?". Before opening the formula sheet, ask yourself the question the topic answers. If you can name the problem, the solution becomes memorable rather than arbitrary.

2. Build intuition with simulations

A static diagram can't show you how a projectile actually flies, or how current flows through a circuit. Interactive simulations let you push the sliders and watch the physics respond. Your brain remembers what it does far better than what it reads.

3. Derive the formula once by hand

Don't memorise "v = u + at". Derive it from the definition of acceleration. When you build a formula from its starting assumptions, it stops being a random symbol soup and becomes a consequence. Formula sheets tempt you to skip this step — don't.

4. Check dimensions and units

After any derivation, sanity-check the units. If you've solved for a velocity but the units come out as metres, something's wrong. Training yourself to check units catches half of all computation mistakes — and it's pure physics understanding.

5. Practice questions that explain themselves

Passive reading feels productive but isn't. Answer questions, get them wrong, and read the explanation. Wrong answers that you understand teach you more than right answers you didn't have to think about.
